SHARED STATUTORY PENALTY TEXTS:
P1: Statutory consequences under the RBI Act 1934: RBI may impose a monetary penalty under s.58G: up to ₹25,000, or, for NBFI business without a CoR (s.58B(4A)) or for accepting deposits or not complying with a Chapter IIIB direction or order (s.58B(5)(a), (aa)), up to ₹10 lakh or twice the amount involved, whichever is more, plus up to ₹1 lakh a day while the default continues; a show-cause notice and hearing come first, and penalty and prosecution are alternatives (s.58G(2), (5)-(6)). As offences, s.58B(5) carries up to 3 years and fine, and s.58B(6) a fine up to ₹1 lakh plus ₹10,000 a day. RBI may also cancel the CoR (s.45-IA(6)), prohibit deposits or bar asset transfers (s.45MB), remove a director (s.45-ID), supersede the Board (s.45-IE) and petition for winding up (s.45MC). For HFCs the parallel provisions are NHB Act ss.29A(6), 49 and 52A (NHB or RBI may impose penalties, e.g. up to ₹10 lakh or twice the amount involved plus ₹1 lakh a day for not complying with a direction or order under Chapter V, s.52A(1C)).
P2: Contraventions of FEMA regulations attract a penalty under FEMA s.13 and may be compounded under s.15.

GLOSSARY (plain-language meanings, not RBI text):
NBFC (Non-Banking Financial Company): A company registered with RBI under Chapter IIIB of the RBI Act whose main business is lending, investing in securities, leasing or similar financial activity, but which is not a bank.
NBFI (Non-Banking Financial Institution): The wider term used in s.45-I of the RBI Act for a financial institution that is not a bank. An NBFC is an NBFI that is a company.
SBR (Scale Based Regulation): RBI's framework that places NBFCs in layers (Base, Middle, Upper and Top) by size, activity and risk. The rules get stricter as you go up, and each layer also follows the rules of the layers below it.
BL (Base Layer): The lowest SBR layer: mainly non-deposit-taking NBFCs below ₹1,000 crore of assets, plus some types (such as P2P platforms, Account Aggregators and NOFHCs) that always stay here. Lightest regulation.
ML (Middle Layer): The SBR layer for larger non-deposit-taking NBFCs (₹1,000 crore of assets or more) and for all deposit-taking NBFCs, HFCs, IFCs, IDF-NBFCs, CICs and SPDs that are not in the Upper Layer.
UL (Upper Layer): The SBR layer for the largest NBFCs: since 24-06-2026, those with assets of ₹1,00,000 crore or more, identified by RBI each year. They face bank-like rules such as Common Equity Tier 1 capital and a listing requirement.
TL (Top Layer): An SBR layer that is kept empty. RBI can move an Upper Layer NBFC into it if it sees a substantial increase in systemic risk.
HFC (Housing Finance Company): An NBFC whose main business is financing housing. It is registered under s.29A of the NHB Act, regulated by RBI and inspected by NHB. HFCs sit in the Middle or Upper Layer.
NHB (National Housing Bank): The statutory body under the NHB Act, 1987 that supervises (inspects) HFCs and handles their complaints, while RBI makes the rules for them.
NBFC-D (Deposit-taking NBFC): An NBFC allowed by RBI to accept public deposits. It is always at least in the Middle Layer and must follow the Public Deposits Directions.
NBFC-ND (Non-deposit-taking NBFC): An NBFC that does not accept public deposits.
NBFC-ICC (Investment and Credit Company): The general NBFC category for companies that lend and/or invest. Most lending NBFCs are ICCs.
NBFC-MFI (Microfinance Institution): An NBFC that holds most of its assets as microfinance loans, i.e. collateral-free loans to low-income households.
NBFC-IFC (Infrastructure Finance Company): An NBFC that deploys most of its assets in infrastructure loans.
IDF-NBFC (Infrastructure Debt Fund NBFC): An NBFC set up to refinance infrastructure projects, mainly through long-term debt.
CIC (Core Investment Company): An NBFC whose business is holding investments in its group companies, not lending to outsiders. Excluded from many lending Directions.
MGC (Mortgage Guarantee Company): An NBFC whose main business is guaranteeing mortgage loans.
NBFC-P2P (Peer to Peer Lending Platform): An NBFC that runs an online platform matching individual lenders and borrowers. It may not lend from its own balance sheet. Always in the Base Layer.
NBFC-AA (Account Aggregator): An NBFC that moves a customer's financial data between institutions with the customer's consent. Always in the Base Layer.
NOFHC (Non-Operative Financial Holding Company): A holding company through which a promoter group holds a bank or other financial entities. Always in the Base Layer.
SPD (Standalone Primary Dealer): An NBFC authorised by RBI to deal in government securities as a primary dealer.
CoR (Certificate of Registration): The registration RBI grants under s.45-IA of the RBI Act (or s.29A of the NHB Act for HFCs) that allows a company to carry on NBFC business. RBI can cancel it.
NOF (Net Owned Fund): Broadly, paid-up capital and free reserves less losses, intangibles and certain investments in group companies. NBFCs must hold a minimum NOF to be registered.
CRAR (Capital to Risk-weighted Assets Ratio): Capital as a percentage of risk-weighted assets; the main measure of whether an NBFC has enough capital for the risks it carries.
NPA (Non-Performing Asset): A loan whose interest or principal is overdue beyond the set period (90 days for most NBFC loans). It must be classified and provided for under the prudential norms.
IRACP (Income Recognition, Asset Classification and Provisioning): The prudential rules on when income may be booked, how loans are classified (standard, NPA and so on) and how much must be set aside as provisions.
SMA (Special Mention Account): An early-warning label for a loan that is overdue but not yet an NPA (SMA-0, SMA-1, SMA-2 by days overdue).
ECL (Expected Credit Loss): A forward-looking way of providing for loan losses (as under Ind AS 109), based on the chance and size of future default.
LTV (Loan to Value ratio): The loan amount as a percentage of the value of the security, e.g. the gold pledged. RBI caps it for some loans.
KYC (Know Your Customer): The rules for identifying and verifying customers and monitoring their accounts to prevent money laundering and terrorist financing.
CKYCR (Central KYC Records Registry): The central database of customers' KYC records. Lenders upload KYC data to it and can fetch existing records when onboarding a customer.
PAN (Permanent Account Number): The income-tax identity number. Lenders must obtain the PAN, or Form 60 if the customer has none, when opening an account-based relationship.
Form 60 (Form 60 (Income-tax Rules)): A declaration given by a person who does not have a PAN, accepted in place of the PAN for KYC.
PMLA (Prevention of Money-laundering Act, 2002): The anti-money-laundering law. NBFCs are reporting entities under it and must report certain transactions to FIU-IND.
FIU-IND (Financial Intelligence Unit – India): The government body that receives cash and suspicious transaction reports from reporting entities under the PMLA.
STR (Suspicious Transaction Report): A report to FIU-IND of a transaction that appears linked to money laundering or other crime.
CTR (Cash Transaction Report): A report to FIU-IND of cash transactions above the set threshold.
IO (Internal Ombudsman): A senior independent officer inside a covered NBFC who must review complaints that the NBFC has partly or wholly rejected before the customer is told the final answer. HFCs, CICs, NBFC-IFCs and IDF-NBFCs are outside the 2026 Directions.
RB-IOS (Reserve Bank – Integrated Ombudsman Scheme): RBI's free complaint scheme for customers of regulated entities, including covered NBFCs, when the entity has not resolved the complaint.
KFS (Key Fact Statement): A short standard sheet given to a borrower before the loan, showing the annual percentage rate, all charges and the repayment schedule.
APR (Annual Percentage Rate): The all-in yearly cost of a loan, including interest and fees, shown in the Key Fact Statement.
LSP (Lending Service Provider): An agent of a lender that carries out functions such as sourcing customers, underwriting support or recovery in digital lending.
DLA (Digital Lending App): A mobile or web app, of the lender or its LSP, through which digital loans are offered.
DLG (Default Loss Guarantee): A promise by a third party (such as an LSP) to cover a lender's losses on a loan portfolio up to a limit. RBI caps it and sets conditions.
Securitisation: Pooling loans and selling them to a special purpose entity, which pays for them by issuing notes (PTCs) to investors who are repaid from the loans' cash flows.
SPE (Special Purpose Entity): A separate trust or company set up only to buy a pool of loans in a securitisation and issue securitisation notes against it, so the assets are kept apart from the originator's own risks.
PTC (Pass-Through Certificate): The usual form of securitisation note in India: investors receive the payments collected on the underlying loans, passed through by the SPE.
Originator (Originator (securitisation)): The lender that made the loans and transfers them to the SPE in a securitisation.
MRR (Minimum Retention Requirement): The share of a securitised pool that the originator must keep, so it keeps 'skin in the game'.
MHP (Minimum Holding Period): The minimum time a lender must hold a loan before it can securitise or transfer it.
Direct assignment (Direct assignment (DA)): Selling loans directly to another lender without an SPE or notes. It falls under the Transfer of Loan Exposures Directions, not the Securitisation Directions.
Dematerialised (Dematerialised (demat) form): Held electronically in a depository account (NSDL or CDSL) instead of as a paper certificate.
Ticket size (Ticket size (securitisation notes)): The amount a single investor puts into securitisation notes. The minimum is ₹1 crore, and from 01-04-2027 it also applies at every transfer.
CERSAI (Central Registry of Securitisation Asset Reconstruction and Security Interest of India): The central registry where security interests must be registered. Under SARFAESI s.26D a lender cannot enforce without registration.
SARFAESI (Securitisation and Reconstruction of Financial Assets and Enforcement of Security Interest Act, 2002): The law that lets notified secured lenders enforce security (e.g. take possession of the property) without going to court, above set loan sizes.
IBC (Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016): The insolvency law. CIRP under it is the process for resolving a company in default.
FEMA (Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999): The foreign-exchange law. Foreign borrowing and lending by NBFCs is governed by regulations made under it.
ECB (External Commercial Borrowing): Borrowing in foreign currency or rupees from lenders outside India. Since 16-02-2026 governed by the FEMA (Borrowing and Lending) Regulations as amended.
SEBI (Securities and Exchange Board of India): The securities regulator. Listed NBFCs and public offers of securities (including securitisation notes) also follow SEBI rules.
CICRA (Credit Information Companies (Regulation) Act, 2005): The law under which lenders must report borrower data to credit bureaus (credit information companies).
PSS (Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007): The law for payment systems such as prepaid instruments and payment aggregators.
PPI (Prepaid Payment Instrument): A wallet or card loaded with money in advance, regulated under the PSS Act.
DPDP (Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023): The data-protection law that governs how lenders collect and use customers' personal data.
CRE (Commercial Real Estate): Loans for office, retail, hotel and similar property whose repayment depends mainly on the property's income or sale.
ALM (Asset Liability Management): Managing the mismatch between when an NBFC's assets mature and when its liabilities fall due.
LCR (Liquidity Coverage Ratio): High-quality liquid assets held against expected net cash outflows over 30 days of stress. It applies to larger NBFCs.
HQLA (High Quality Liquid Assets): Assets such as government securities that can quickly be turned into cash, counted for the LCR.
ICAAP (Internal Capital Adequacy Assessment Process): A Board-approved internal assessment of how much capital the NBFC needs for all its risks. Required for Middle and Upper Layer NBFCs.
PCA (Prompt Corrective Action): RBI's framework that restricts an NBFC's activities when capital, asset quality or other measures breach set thresholds.
CCO (Chief Compliance Officer): The senior officer responsible for the NBFC's compliance function, required for Middle and Upper Layer NBFCs.
RBIA (Risk-Based Internal Audit): An internal audit approach that focuses effort on the highest-risk areas, required for larger NBFCs.
Co-lending: A bank and an NBFC jointly lending to a borrower under a prior agreement, sharing the loan in agreed proportions.
EBT (Electronic Banking Transaction): A payment made through digital banking channels such as internet or mobile banking, UPI or cards.
D-SIB (Domestic Systemically Important Bank): A bank so large and connected that RBI applies extra capital requirements to it.
AIFI (All India Financial Institution): Development finance institutions such as NABARD, SIDBI, EXIM Bank and NHB, regulated by RBI under their own Directions.
SFB (Small Finance Bank): A bank licensed to serve small borrowers and businesses, with its own RBI Directions.

RBI UPDATES IN THE LAST 7 DAYS (2026-11-18 to 2026-11-25):
- 2026-11-23 Amendment Directions: Reserve Bank of India (Non-Banking Financial Companies – Securitisation Transactions) Amendment Directions, 2026 (RBI/2026-27/306; DOR.STR.REC.258/21-04-177/2026-27). Applies: NBFCs covered by para 3 of the NBFC Securitisation Transactions Directions 2025: NBFC-D, NBFC-ICC, NBFC-Factor, NBFC-MFI, NBFC-IFC, IDF-NBFC and HFCs (para 3(vii)), all layers. Outside para 3: CICs, MGCs, NBFC-P2P, NBFC-AA, NOFHCs, SPDs and Type I NBFCs, so an Upper Layer CIC is not covered as originator. Direct assignments of loans fall under the separate Transfer of Loan Exposures Directions and are not affected. The same changes were made on the same day for commercial banks, SFBs and AIFIs (RBI/2026-27/304, 305, 307).. Changes: para 26 (substituted): the issuance, holding and subsequent transfer of securitisation notes must be in dematerialised form only. The ₹1 crore minimum ticket size applies at issue and at every later transfer. The originator must ensure that its agreement with the SPE requires the SPE to put in place arrangements with entities such as the registrar, depository and stock exchange, "as may be applicable", to secure the minimum ticket size. Proviso: notes with amortisation structures may be traded at amortised value if the ticket size falls below ₹1 crore. Explanation: ticket size is the investment of a single investor. para 27 (substituted): for an offer of securitisation notes to the public, Regulation 21 of the SEBI (Issue and Listing of Securitised Debt Instruments and Security Receipts) Regulations 2008 must be complied with. RBI's own earlier wording (listing "recommended, though not mandatory"; offers to fifty or more persons to be listed) is removed, so the SEBI Regulations now decide what is a public offer. para 27A (new): transactions in notes issued before 01-04-2027 must also comply with paras 26 and 27. There is no grandfathering for older notes. Effective 01-04-2027 (para 3). Press release 2026-2027/1550 says this finalises the draft of 27-07-2026 after comments. Actions: List outstanding securitisation notes where the entity is originator or investor, with their form (physical or demat) and ticket sizes. Flag positions below ₹1 crore that are not amortising, because they may become non-transferable from 01-04-2027. With trustees and SPEs, plan dematerialisation and the ticket-size arrangements. Check what the trust deeds allow and whose consent is needed to amend them. Seek clarity, for example through an industry body, on whether physical notes held to maturity without any transaction must be dematerialised. For any offer that may be a public offer, check it against SEBI Regulation 21. Update the securitisation policy and brief the Board or risk committee.
- 2026-11-20 Enforcement (press release): RBI imposes monetary penalty on Fusion Finance Limited (Press Release 2026-2027/1537 (order dated 17-11-2026)). Applies: Another lender (described as an NBFC in secondary sources). It is a routine enforcement order and creates no new rule.. Changes: Penalty of ₹7.50 lakh by order of 17-11-2026, under RBI Act s.58G(1)(b) read with s.58B(5)(aa), following a statutory inspection of the position as on 31-03-2026. Findings: the company failed to obtain PAN (or its e-document) or Form 60 when establishing account-based relationships, and failed to escalate partly or wholly rejected complaints to the Internal Ombudsman within the prescribed time. RBI cites its KYC and Internal Ombudsman directions without paragraph numbers. Actions: Customer-facing entities: sample-test new account openings for PAN or Form 60. NBFCs covered by the Internal Ombudsman Directions only: check that every partly or wholly rejected complaint reached the Internal Ombudsman in time, with an audit trail.
- 2026-11-23 Meeting (press release): Governor meets MDs and CEOs of select NBFCs, HFCs and industry bodies (Press Release 2026-2027/1554). Applies: Select NBFCs (including Government NBFCs), HFCs and MFIs. Sa-Dhan, MFIN and FIDC took part, and the NHB MD & CEO attended. No new directions were issued.. Changes: The Governor stressed responsible and sustainable credit growth, fair recovery practices, use of CKYCR for onboarding, and a comprehensive, risk-based approach to cyber and IT risk. Actions: Check CKYCR upload and download practice at onboarding, and recovery-agent conduct controls.
- 2026-11-23 Draft Amendment Directions (for comment): Draft Amendment Directions on 'Preventive Safeguards for Electronic Banking Transactions (EBTs)' (Press Release 2026-2027/1548). Applies: Seven drafts amending the Responsible Business Conduct Directions for commercial banks, SFBs, payments banks, LABs, RRBs, UCBs and RCBs. No NBFC or HFC draft. Comments due by 14-12-2026.. Changes: Seven draft amendments to the banks' Responsible Business Conduct Directions. Per the press release, they aim to give customers greater control over how and how far their digital payment channels can be used, and to widen the definition of "negligence by a bank" in the 24-06-2026 customer-liability amendment. The press release does not describe the detailed proposals; they are in the seven draft PDFs, which were not read here. Actions: Read the draft PDFs and, if loan-collection or co-lending flows would be hit, comment through an industry body by 14-12-2026.
